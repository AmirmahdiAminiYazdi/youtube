## ۳. توضیحات

```
How a water well works, built step by step: borehole, casing, grout seal, well screen, submersible pump and pressure tank. Each part fixes the problem the last one created.

More than 43 million people in the US get their drinking water from private wells (USGS, EPA).

The steps in the video:
1. Borehole: drilled down to an aquifer, a layer of sand, gravel or rock that holds water.
2. Casing: a steel or plastic pipe that stops loose ground from caving in.
3. Grout seal: cement or clay in the gap around the casing, so surface water can't run down the outside.
4. Well screen: a slotted section that lets water in and holds sand back, used in sandy ground.
5. Submersible pump: sits below the water level and pushes water up.
6. Pressure tank: stores water under pressure so the pump starts less often.

Simplified on purpose: the order and timing of the parts vary by region and driller, many bedrock wells have no screen, and small parts like the well cap, pitless adapter, check valve and pressure switch are left out. "Before dinner" is a joke, not how long drilling takes.

Real wells are built by licensed drillers under local rules. This is a simplified explanation, not a how-to.

Narration is an AI-generated voice. The 3D animation was made by Brainmaxx.

Sources: USGS Water Science School, "Aquifers and Groundwater"; USGS, "What's in Your Well Water?"; US EPA, "About Private Water Wells" and Private Well module; University of Arizona Cooperative Extension, "Private Water Well Components" (AZ1486b, AZ1504); University of Nebraska-Lincoln Extension G2152; Washington State WAC 173-160-231.

Brainmaxx: Think better. Learn faster.
```
