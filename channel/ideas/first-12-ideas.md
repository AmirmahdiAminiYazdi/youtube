# Channel plan and first 12 Shorts

Language of the videos: English. Format: vertical 9:16 Shorts, cross-posted to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels. Visuals: motion graphics built with Claude (HTML + GSAP, rendered to video). Faceless.

## Channel promise (one sentence, used everywhere)

"Think better and learn faster, with AI and with your own brain."

Three fixed series, so viewers know what they are subscribing to:

| Series | What it is | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| **AI in 60** | One AI skill or tool, one result, in about 60 seconds. Always shown on screen. | Complete beginners |
| **Mind Hacks** | One well-established psychology idea, tied to a daily-life moment. | Everyone |
| **Learn Faster** | One research-backed study or thinking technique. | Students, self-learners |

Why these three belong together: AI in 60 teaches the tool, Learn Faster teaches how to learn with it, Mind Hacks explains why we get in our own way. Same viewer, same promise.

## Rules that protect the channel

1. **Each video needs its own central visual metaphor** (a lawyer vs a judge, a leaking bucket, a spotlight). Keep the colors, fonts and motion style the same, and change the idea in the picture every time. YouTube's July 2026 clarification targets generic, template-style content that varies little between videos.
2. **Every script has one original element:** a live on-screen demo, a worked example, or a clear point of view. No read-aloud summaries.
3. **No invented numbers, results or sources.** Every claim in Mind Hacks and Learn Faster is checked against the cited source before recording. The citations below are from memory and must be verified.
4. **No diagnosing, no medical or financial advice.** Psychology is explained as general ideas, never applied to the viewer as a condition.
5. **Animation needs no AI label** in YouTube Studio when it is clearly not realistic footage. Do not use photoreal AI scenes presented as real.

## The 12 ideas

Scores come from `yt-script/hookscore.py` (a rough pattern heuristic, 55 and above is WORKABLE, 72 and above is STRONG). None is STRONG yet. Real feedback comes from retention data after posting.

### AI in 60

1. **3 Lines That Make AI Useful** (67)
   Hook: "Before you give up on AI, try these 3 lines. Nobody tells beginners this."
   Visual: three lines slide in one by one (who you are, what you need, what the answer should look like), then before/after of the same question.
   Original element: run the exact same question with and without the lines, on screen.

2. **Make AI Quiz You** (56)
   Hook: "Stop asking AI for answers. One prompt turns it into a tutor that quizzes you before you forget."
   Visual: a chat bubble flips into a quiz card. Show the prompt.
   Original element: quiz yourself live on a real topic.

3. **Catch AI Lying in 10 Seconds** (69)
   Hook: "How do you catch AI lying before it wastes your day? It takes 10 seconds."
   Visual: confident answer, then a magnifier ticks or crosses each claim.
   Original element: ask AI for a source, then check one real claim on screen. Show a real mistake you found.

4. **Voice Note to Plan** (47, rewrite the hook before recording)
   Hook draft: "Never stare at a blank page again. Talk for 60 seconds, and AI writes the plan. Here's how."
   Visual: sound wave turns into a checklist.
   Original element: use a real messy voice note.

### Mind Hacks

5. **The If-Then Trick** (55)
   Hook: "Before you quit your next goal, try this one-sentence format."
   Concept: implementation intentions, "If X happens, then I will Y." Check: Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006, meta-analysis.
   Visual: a fork in the road that auto-selects a path.

6. **Why You're Always Late** (58)
   Hook: "Why do you always run late, even when you plan ahead? Before you blame yourself, learn this."
   Concept: planning fallacy. Check: Kahneman and Tversky, 1979; Buehler, Griffin and Ross, 1994.
   Visual: a plan bar on a timeline that keeps stretching past its deadline.

7. **The Spotlight Effect** (54)
   Hook: "Stop wasting sleep on that embarrassing moment. Almost nobody noticed as much as you think."
   Concept: spotlight effect. Check: Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 2000. State the finding as the paper does.
   Visual: a person under a huge spotlight, the crowd looking elsewhere.

8. **Your Brain Is a Lawyer** (55)
   Hook: "Nobody told you your brain is a lawyer, not a judge. It never argues the other side."
   Concept: confirmation bias and motivated reasoning. Check: Nickerson, 1998, review.
   Visual: a courtroom where evidence only flows to one side.

### Learn Faster

9. **Stop Rereading** (52, rewrite the hook before recording)
   Hook draft: "Why does rereading feel like studying, but fail you on test day? Stop doing it."
   Concept: rereading is rated low utility, practice testing high. Check: Dunlosky et al., 2013.
   Visual: a highlighted page fading versus flashcards locking in.

10. **When to Review** (55)
    Hook: "You lose new knowledge fast, except when you review it at the right time. Here's when."
    Concept: spacing effect. Check: Cepeda et al., 2006. Do not give exact day counts unless the source states them.
    Visual: a leaking bucket refilled at spaced moments.

11. **The 10-Year-Old Test** (69)
    Hook: "Before you say you understand it, explain it to a 10-year-old. Where you stumble is what you don't know yet."
    Concept: explaining as a check of understanding, popularly attributed to Feynman. Say "popularly called the Feynman technique", do not claim he named it.
    Visual: a simple diagram that breaks at the exact spot where the explanation fails.

12. **Learning Styles Are a Myth** (52, rewrite the hook before recording)
    Hook draft: "Stop calling yourself a visual learner. Why the research actually doesn't back it."
    Concept: learning-styles matching has little supporting evidence. Check: Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer and Bjork, 2008.
    Visual: four labeled doors that all open to the same room.

| Week | AI in 60 | Mind Hacks | Learn Faster |

After week 4 compare the three series by saves, completion rate and shares, then give the winning series more slots. Adjust the cadence to the hours you actually have.

## Per-platform notes

- Length: 45 to 60 seconds. Make one 61 to 75 second cut for TikTok if you want to test its longer-video rewards.
- Burn captions into the master. Many people watch muted.
- Export a clean master with no watermark, post it on all three, and stagger the times by a few hours. Instagram may deprioritize a Reel it recognizes as posted elsewhere first.
- First 1.5 seconds: the hook as big on-screen text plus the voice.
