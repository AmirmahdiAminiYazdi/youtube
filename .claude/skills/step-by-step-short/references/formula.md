## Beat map (airplane, 6 steps shown; timing is for a 55 s version)

| Time | Beat | What the viewer hears | What the viewer sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Hook | "You're stranded on an island. You bolt a boat engine onto a wooden crate. Congratulations!" | Frame 0: two big lines ("ONE CRATE. ONE ENGINE."), the crate with its pill, already on screen |
| 3-8 | Problem 1 | "It rolls, but it can't lift." | HUD: STEP 1 / 6, PROBLEM: NO LIFT |
| 8-12 | Part 1 | "So you strap a surfboard on each side. Those are wings." | wings pop on the word "wings", pill WING, burst |
| ... | Chain | each step: problem, then part, then real name | same pattern, camera pushes in a little, the build grows |
| mid | Analogy | "...an upright fin, like the feathers on an arrow." | the fin pops, pill VERTICAL STABILIZER |
| late | Payoff | "You built an airplane. The seagull is not impressed." | payoff title, camera pulls back and orbits, one gag |
| last 3 | Ask + loop | "Save this for the next time someone asks how planes fly." | end card; last 0.15 s equals frame 0 |

## Why each element is there (hypotheses to test)

- Second-person scenario: stakes without claims; viewers picture themselves in it.
- Wrong base object: a cheap, funny reason to watch the fix.
- Problem-then-part: every step opens a small loop (what will fix this?), closed 4-6 s later; that is the retention mechanism.
- Real names on pills: teaches a new word at the moment its job is clear.
- Step counter: shows progress, so people stay for "STEP 6 / 6".
- Loop frame: replays count toward average % viewed above 100 %.

## What is NOT part of the formula

Do not copy the airplane's content, voice or scenes into other topics. Channel rule 1 (first-12-ideas.md): every video needs its own central visual metaphor; here the metaphor is the **object being built**, so it changes every time. Keep palette, type and motion language fixed; change the object.
